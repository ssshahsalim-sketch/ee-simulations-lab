Comprehensive Core Loss Theory & Mathematics

Detailed explanation of Electromagnetic Induction, Eddy Currents, Lamination Physics, and Hysteresis Loss.

1. What are Eddy Currents?

According to Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction ($\mathcal{E} = -d\Phi/dt$), an alternating magnetic flux passing through a conducting metallic core induces an electromotive force (EMF) inside the metal body.

Since bulk metals have low electrical resistance, these induced voltages create circulating loops of current within the body of the conductor, known as eddy currents (due to their swirl-like resemblance to water eddies).

P_e = \frac{\pi^2 \cdot f^2 \cdot B_m^2 \cdot t^2}{6 \cdot \rho \cdot D} \quad \text{(W/kg)}
  • $f$: Frequency of alternating magnetic field (Hz)
  • $B_m$: Peak magnetic flux density (Tesla)
  • $t$: Lamination thickness (meters)
  • $\rho$: Material electrical resistivity ($\Omega\cdot\text{m}$)
  • $D$: Material density ($\text{kg/m}^3$)
2. How Lamination Reduces Eddy Loss

In transformers and electric motors, the core is constructed from thin sheets of silicon steel (e.g., CRGO - Cold Rolled Grain Oriented steel), stacked together and insulated from each other by a thin layer of varnish or oxide enamel.

The insulation planes are oriented parallel to the magnetic flux lines. This presents a very high electrical resistance to the induced eddy current paths without obstructing the flow of magnetic flux!

\text{Loss Reduction Factor} = \left(\frac{1}{N}\right)^2

If a solid core is split into $N=10$ laminations, the loss drops to $1/100^{\text{th}}$ ($1\%$) of the solid core value.

3. Hysteresis Loss & Steinmetz Law

Ferromagnetic materials consist of microscopic magnetic domains. When subject to AC magnetic fields, these domains must continually reorient themselves back and forth.

Domain wall movement experiences internal magnetic friction and lag (hysteresis). The energy lost per unit volume during each cycle equals the area enclosed by the B-H Hysteresis Loop.

P_h = \eta \cdot f \cdot B_m^{1.6} \cdot V \quad \text{(Steinmetz Formula)}
  • $\eta$: Steinmetz hysteresis coefficient
  • $B_m^{1.6}$: Empirical magnetic density exponent (typically 1.6 to 2.0)
  • $V$: Volume of magnetic core ($\text{m}^3$)
4. Real-World Engineering Applications
  • Induction Heating / Furnaces: High-frequency eddy currents are deliberately generated in metal workpieces to melt or heat-treat metals rapidly without physical contact.
  • Eddy Current Braking (Maglev & Trains): Strong electromagnets induce eddy currents in metal discs/rails, creating smooth, non-wearing braking forces (Lenz's Law).
  • Powdered Iron & Ferrite Cores: At radio frequencies (MHz), laminations are too thick, so magnetic cores are made of microscopic iron particles suspended in insulating resin.
