What is Six Sigma? (In Plain Words)

At its core, **Six Sigma** is a disciplined, data-driven approach to eliminate defects and reduce variation in any process—whether manufacturing electrical control panels, processing insurance claims, or assembling motors.

Six Sigma Convention Note (The 1.5σ Shift): In standard industry practice, Six Sigma assumes process shifts by up to 1.5 standard deviations over time due to wear, raw material batches, and environmental changes. A 6-Sigma process generates 3.4 Defects Per Million Opportunities (DPMO) long-term.

Why Six Sigma? Reducing Variation is Key

Consider a copper busbar cutting process with a target length of 100.0 mm and specification tolerance of 98.0 mm to 102.0 mm. Compare three factories below:

Process A (Low Sigma ~ 2σ)

Wide spread. Many parts fall outside specification limit lines.

Process B (3σ to 4σ)

Moderate spread. Few defects, but minor process shifts create scrap.

Process C (Six Sigma ~ 6σ)

Ultra-narrow variation. Entire process fits safely inside limits!

🏭 Real Industrial Case Studies

Detailed DMAIC breakdowns across electrical engineering, automotive, and healthcare sectors.

ELECTRICAL PANEL MFG

Reducing Terminal Loose Torque Defects

Problem: 8.2% of MCCB panels failed high-current testing due to loose screw terminations.
Root Cause: Operators relied on manual screwdriver feel without torque drivers.
Improvement: Calibrated pneumatic torque wrenches with poka-yoke shutoff.
Financial Benefit: $142,000 saved per year in rework costs.

MOTOR MANUFACTURING

Stator Winding Resistance Variation

Problem: Cpk of motor winding resistance was 0.72.
Root Cause: Copper wire tensioning variation during automatic spooling.
Improvement: Implemented SPC closed-loop tension control. Cpk reached 1.84.
Financial Benefit: Reduced scrap rate from 4.1% to 0.03%.
