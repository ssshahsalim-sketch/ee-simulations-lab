1. DC Motor Fundamentals

Simple Explanation

A DC motor is an electrical machine that converts direct current (DC) electrical energy into rotating mechanical power. Think of it like a magnet pushing against an electromagnet—by continuously switching the electric current, we keep the rotor spinning forever!

Technical Engineering Explanation

When a current-carrying conductor is immersed in a stationary magnetic field produced by the stator, Lorentz electromagnetic force (F = B·I·L·sinθ) acts on the conductor. Due to the armature symmetry, these forces form an electromagnetic torque pair (Te = k·Φ·Ia). As the rotor turns, the mechanical commutator flips the armature current polarity every half-cycle to maintain unidirectional torque.

Mathematical Derivation

Electromagnetic Force: F = B · I · L · sin(θ)
Electromagnetic Torque: Te = k · Φ · Ia
Back EMF Equation: Eb = (P · Z · Φ · N) / (60 · A) = k · Φ · ω
Armature Voltage Balance: V = Eb + Ia · Ra

Common Misconception

"Back EMF is a loss of energy." FALSE! Back EMF is actually the electrical reflection of mechanical power output (Pmech = Eb · Ia). Without Back EMF, a motor would draw infinite current and burn out!

Key Engineering Rule

NEVER start a large DC motor directly across full line voltage without starting resistance or a soft starter, because at speed N=0, back EMF Eb=0, resulting in a dangerous starting current spike Ia = V / Ra (up to 10-20x rated current)!

1. Lorentz Conductor Force
F = B · I · L · sin(θ)
B: Flux density (T), I: Current (A), L: Length (m)
2. Electromagnetic Torque
Te = k · Φ · Ia
k = (P·Z)/(2π·A), Φ: Flux (Wb), Ia: Armature Current
3. Back EMF Equation
Eb = k · Φ · ω
ω = 2π·N/60 (rad/s), N: Speed (RPM)
4. Voltage Balance Equation
V = Eb + Ia · (Ra + Radj)
V: Supply, Eb: Back EMF, Ra: Armature Resistance
5. Speed Equation
N ∝ (V - Ia·Ra) / Φ
Speed is directly proportional to Eb and inversely to Φ
6. Power & Efficiency
Pout = Te · ω | Pin = V · I_total
η = (Pout / Pin) × 100%
