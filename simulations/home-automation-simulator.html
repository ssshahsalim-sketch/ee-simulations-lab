System Architecture ESP32 core

One ESP32 dev board reads sensors on its GPIO/ADC pins and drives a relay module to switch mains-powered loads. Everything talks to your phone over Wi-Fi — no wired connection to the router needed.

Power plan

Two isolated power domains — keep them separate.

DomainSupply
ESP32 + sensors5V/2A USB
Relay coils5V (from ESP32 5V rail, opto-isolated)
Relay loads230V AC mains
⚠ The relay module's opto-isolator is what keeps 230V mains electrically separated from the 3.3V ESP32 logic. Always confirm your relay board has opto-isolation before wiring it directly to microcontroller GPIO — never switch mains with a bare relay coil driven straight off a signal pin.

Wiring order (avoids most beginner mistakes)

  1. Wire and test each sensor alone first — confirm readings on Serial Monitor before adding the next one.
  2. Wire the relay board's logic side (VCC/GND/IN pins) only — leave mains side untouched.
  3. Confirm each relay clicks and its status LED changes when toggled from code.
  4. Only then connect the mains side, with power off, inside the enclosure.
  5. Move from Serial Monitor testing to the app (Blynk/Home Assistant) last.

Safety notes

Worth repeating since this is the part most hobbyist guides skip:

  • Never work on the relay's mains-side terminals while powered.
  • Use an opto-isolated relay board — non-isolated boards exist and are not safe for direct GPIO switching of mains loads.
  • Fully enclose any mains wiring; keep the low-voltage ESP32 side accessible for reprogramming.
  • Add a fuse or MCB ahead of the relay's mains input if switching anything above a small lamp load.
