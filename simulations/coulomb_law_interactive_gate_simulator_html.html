What is Electric Charge? Level 1

Electric charge is the fundamental intrinsic property of matter that causes it to experience a force when placed in an electromagnetic field. Charges are quantized ($q = n \cdot e$) and strictly conserved.

Elementary Charge (e) 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
Electron Mass (mₑ) 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg
Proton Mass (mₚ) 1.672 × 10⁻²⁷ kg

GATE Special Symmetry Cases & Zero-Force Equilibrium Level 7
1. Square Corners

4 equal charges $q$ at corners of square side $a$. Force on any corner charge:

$$F_{\text{net}} = \frac{k q^2}{a^2} \left( \sqrt{2} + \frac{1}{2} \right)$$
2. Equilateral Triangle

3 equal charges $q$ at vertices of triangle side $a$. Force on any vertex charge:

$$F_{\text{net}} = \sqrt{3} \frac{k q^2}{a^2}$$
3. Center Symmetry

Equal charges placed symmetrically at vertices of any regular polygon exert ZERO net force on a central charge $Q_0$:

$$\vec{F}_{\text{center}} = \vec{0}$$
Equilibrium Position Calculation (2 Charges Line)

For two charges $q_1$ and $q_2$ separated by distance $L$, a third charge $q_3$ feels zero net force at position $x$ from $q_1$:

Same Polarity (q₁ and q₂ same sign) x = L / ( 1 + √(q₂/q₁) ) [Between charges]
Opposite Polarity (|q₁| < |q₂|) x = L / ( √(q₂/q₁) - 1 ) [Outside smaller charge]

Engineering & Industrial Applications Level 9
1. Electrostatic Precipitator

High voltage corona wire ionizes flue gas. Negatively charged ash particles experience Coulomb attraction towards grounded collection plates, removing >99% particulate pollution in power plants.

2. Photocopier / Laser Printer

Photoconductive drum is electrostatically charged. Laser discharges selected areas. Negatively charged toner powder is attracted via Coulomb force to positively charged drum areas and paper.

3. Electrostatic Spray Painting

Atomized paint droplets are given a high negative potential. The target workpiece is grounded. Electrostatic attraction wraps paint evenly around complex 3D metal geometries with minimal overspray.

⚠️ GATE Common Mistakes & Traps
❌ Trap #1: Forgetting r²
Dividing by $r$ instead of $r^2$ when calculating force ratios under time pressure.
❌ Trap #2: Scalar Addition of Vectors
Adding force magnitudes directly ($F_{\text{net}} = F_1 + F_2$) instead of resolving $x$ and $y$ vector components.
❌ Trap #3: Unit Conversion Errors
Leaving charges in $\mu\text{C}$ ($10^{-6}$) or distances in $\text{cm}$ ($10^{-2}$) without converting to SI base units ($\text{C}$, $\text{m}$).
