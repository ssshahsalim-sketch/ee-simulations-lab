Coulomb's Law & Superposition

Each placed charge produces a radial field. The net field at any point is the vector sum (superposition) of every charge's individual contribution:

E = K · q / r²  (direction: along the line from charge to point) E_net(P) = Σ E_i(P)  for all charges i

This simulator uses K = 8.99 with charge values entered directly in nanocoulombs (nC) and distance in metres, so K already folds in the 10⁻⁹ scale factor of ε₀ — the numbers you see for E (N/C) and V (volts) are physically realistic for nC-scale charges a few metres apart.

Electric Potential

Potential is a scalar, so it sums directly (no direction to combine):

V(P) = Σ K · q_i / r_i

The sensor tool reads both E (magnitude + direction) and V at its position live as you drag it through the field.

Field Lines

Field lines are traced by stepping repeatedly in the direction of the local E vector, starting just outside each positive charge. By convention they originate on positive charges and terminate on negative charges (or run to infinity / the edge of the canvas if unbalanced). Density of lines around a charge indicates field strength — closer spacing means a stronger field.

Equipotential Lines

An equipotential line is a path of constant V. Because E is always perpendicular to equipotential surfaces, the tracer walks tangentially (perpendicular to the local E vector) while applying a small Newton correction back onto the target V value at every step — this is why the resulting curve never crosses a field line at anything but a right angle.

step ⟂ E, then correct: Δposition += (V_target − V(P)) / |E|² · E

Reading The Vector Grid

Each arrow's direction shows the net field direction at that grid point; its length and brightness (on a square-root scale) show relative magnitude. Very close to a charge, magnitude is clamped for readability — the true field is unbounded (1/r²) right at the charge.

Why This Matters In The Field

  • Same superposition principle used when analysing stray-field coupling between conductors or busbars
  • Equipotential thinking underlies step/touch-voltage analysis in earthing design (IS 3043 / IEEE 80)
  • Field-line crowding visualises where local field stress — and dielectric breakdown risk — is highest, same concept used in HV insulation/corona assessment
