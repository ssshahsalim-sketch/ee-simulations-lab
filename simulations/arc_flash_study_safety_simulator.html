Why is Arcing Current ($I_{arc}$) lower than Bolted Fault Current ($I_{bf}$)?

An electric arc introduces a dynamic non-linear plasma impedance $Z_{arc}$ into the fault circuit. This additional resistance reduces the total RMS current flowing through the circuit. As a critical consequence, protective devices (breakers/fuses) may trip in their inverse-time region rather than instantaneous region, significantly increasing clearing time and resulting in severe arc-flash energy release!

Step-by-Step Engineering Calculation Math Panel

Full mathematical derivation for short-circuit, arcing current, and incident energy.

1. Short Circuit Impedance Breakdown

Utility Impedance ($Z_{util}$): $Z_{util} = \frac{V_{sec}^2}{S_{sc}} = \frac{0.415^2}{250} = 0.000688 \, \Omega$

Transformer Impedance ($Z_{tx}$): $Z_{tx} = \frac{\%Z}{100} \cdot \frac{V_{sec}^2}{S_{tx}} = 0.0575 \cdot \frac{0.415^2}{1.0} = 0.009903 \, \Omega$

Total Impedance ($Z_{total}$): $Z_{total} = Z_{util} + Z_{tx} + Z_{cable} = 0.01059 \, \Omega$

Bolted Fault Current ($I_{bf}$): $I_{bf} = \frac{V_{sec}}{\sqrt{3} \cdot Z_{total}} = \frac{415}{1.732 \cdot 0.01059} = 22.62 \, \text{kA}$

2. Arcing Current Derivation (IEEE 1584 Fit)

For LV Enclosed Switchgear ($V \le 1.0\,\text{kV}$):

$\log_{10}(I_{arc}) = K + 0.662 \cdot \log_{10}(I_{bf}) + 0.0966 \cdot V + 0.000526 \cdot G$

Calculated $I_{arc} = 16.38 \, \text{kA}$

3. Incident Energy & Arc Flash Boundary

Incident Energy ($E$): $E = 4.184 \cdot C_f \cdot E_n \cdot \left(\frac{t}{0.2}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{610^x}{D^x}\right) = 7.82 \, \text{cal/cm}^2$

Arc Flash Boundary ($D_B$): $D_B = \sqrt[x]{\frac{E_{610}}{1.2}} \cdot 610 = 1820 \, \text{mm} \, (1.82 \, \text{m})$
