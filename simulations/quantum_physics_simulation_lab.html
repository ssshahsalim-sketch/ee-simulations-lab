How Quantum Physics Works

Classical mechanics treats objects as particles with definite positions and trajectories. Quantum mechanics works fundamentally differently:

  • Quantum State Wavefunction $\psi(x)$: Systems are described by probability amplitudes, not continuous mechanical trajectories.
  • Superposition: Before measurement, a quantum system can exist in a linear combination of basis states.
  • Measurement & Probabilities: $|\psi(x)|^2$ gives the probability density of observing outcome $x$ when measured.
  • Entanglement: Multi-qubit states can share non-local correlations that exceed any classical hidden-variable model.
