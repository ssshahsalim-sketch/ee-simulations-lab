Reference & Field Notes

Background notes to help structure fair, transparent wiring quotations. Treat rates as indicative starting points only — always confirm current local material and labor costs before quoting.

What is a "wiring point"?

In Indian residential/commercial electrical trade practice, a "point" is the standard unit for pricing wiring labor — it typically covers running the wire from the distribution board/junction to the outlet location, the switch/socket box, and termination, but not the fixture, switch, or socket itself unless specifically included. Wiremen usually quote per point rather than per meter of wire, since it bundles labor, minor material (clips, tape, small connectors) and site variability into one predictable unit.

Common point categories

CategoryTypical Points
LightingCeiling/wall light point, fan point, exhaust fan point, chandelier/heavy fixture point (extra support wiring)
Power (Plug) Points5A/6A light-duty socket, 15A/16A power socket, AC point (dedicated circuit + isolator), geyser point (dedicated circuit)
Data / CommunicationTV/cable point, telephone point, LAN/computer data point, call bell point
Board & DistributionDB wiring per way/circuit, MCB/RCCB installation, submeter connection, earthing point

Why AC and geyser points cost more

AC and geyser points usually need a dedicated circuit (separate MCB, heavier gauge wire — commonly 2.5–4 sq mm copper for AC, sometimes higher for larger units), a dedicated isolator switch near the appliance, and often a longer concealed run back to the board. This is why they're priced meaningfully above a standard light or fan point rather than treated as a normal socket.

Structuring a transparent bill

  • Separate labor from material. Customers trust a bill more when they can see the wiring/labor charge per point and the material cost separately, rather than one lump sum.
  • Be explicit about who supplies what. If the customer buys the fan, light fixture, or AC themselves, say so on the bill — it explains why those items show ₹0 charge instead of looking like an omission.
  • State a fair markup on material you supply (commonly a modest percentage over your purchase cost to cover sourcing, transport, and handling) rather than leaving customers to guess your margin.
  • Note warranty terms. Workmanship warranty (your labor) and material warranty (manufacturer's, passed through) are different — stating both avoids disputes later.
  • Wire gauge matters for safety, not just cost. As a general reference, lighting circuits commonly use 1.5 sq mm, general sockets 2.5 sq mm, and higher-load circuits like AC/geyser 4 sq mm or above — always size to the actual connected load and local wiring code practice, not price alone.

Using this tool

  1. Set up your Rate Book once with your real labor rates and common equipment prices.
  2. For each job, open New Bill, add the customer's points and equipment (rates/prices are editable per job since sites vary).
  3. Mark each equipment line as supplied by you or the customer — only your-supply items get billed.
  4. Save the bill, then use Bill Preview to download a PDF or print a clean copy to hand to the customer.
  5. All bills stay in Saved Bills for your records — everything is stored locally in this browser (no internet/server needed), so keep this file safe and consider exporting/backing up periodically.
