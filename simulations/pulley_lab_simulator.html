Equilibrium Analysis

$\Sigma F_y = 0$
$n \cdot T - W = 0$
$T = W / n$

For the load to remain in equilibrium or move at a constant velocity, the total upward tension forces from all $n$ supporting segments must equal the downward gravitational weight ($W = mg$).

Gravitational Weight ($W$)
$W = m \cdot g$
$m$ = mass (kg), $g$ = gravity (m/s²)
Ideal Mechanical Advantage (IMA)
$IMA = n = \frac{d_{input}}{d_{load}}$
$n$ = number of supporting rope segments
Actual Mechanical Advantage (AMA)
$AMA = \frac{F_{load}}{F_{input\_real}} = \eta \cdot IMA$
$\eta$ = mechanical efficiency factor
Required Pull Force ($F_{in}$)
$F_{in} = \frac{W}{n \cdot \eta}$
Includes real friction losses
Conservation of Work/Energy
$W_{in} = F_{in} \cdot d_{in}$
$W_{out} = W_{load} \cdot d_{load}$
$W_{in} = W_{out} + W_{loss}$
Ceiling Anchor Support Load
$F_{anchor} = W + F_{in}$ (For downward pull)
$F_{anchor} = W - F_{in}$ (For upward pull)

Engineering Safety Principles

  • Safety Factor ($SF$): Industrial overhead lifting mandates a 5:1 or 10:1 safety factor above maximum breaking strength.
  • Anchor Reaction: Remember that overhead ceiling anchors must support BOTH the load weight AND the reaction force of the input pull.
  • High Mechanical Advantage Risk: A high MA system makes heavy loads easy to pull, which can deceptively overload rigging lines without feeling heavy to the operator!

🚢 Crane & Construction Hoist

Uses heavy multi-sheave block & tackles inside crane booms to enable electric motors to lift multi-ton steel beams effortlessly.

⛵ Sailing Yacht Main Sheet

Sailboats use 4:1 to 8:1 block and tackles to allow human sailors to pull in high-tension sails against gale forces.

⛰️ Mountain & Crevasse Rescue

Rescuers build 3:1 (Z-rig) or 5:1 friction-saving pulley setups with static ropes to pull fallen climbers out of crevasses.

💡 Common Pulley Myths Debunked

MYTH 1: "Adding more pulley wheels always multiplies force."

FALSE!

Mechanical advantage is determined strictly by the number of supporting rope segments bearing the moving load, not merely how many wheels exist in the frame.

MYTH 2: "Pulleys give you free energy."

FALSE!

Conservation of energy ($Work = Force \times Distance$) is absolute. You trade force for distance: a 4:1 pulley reduces pull force to 25%, but you must pull 4x more rope length!
