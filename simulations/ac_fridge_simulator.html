By default: a split AC has two halves joined by insulated copper pipes carrying refrigerant — the outdoor unit (compressor, condenser coil, condenser fan) rejects heat to the atmosphere, and the indoor unit (evaporator coil, blower fan, filter) absorbs heat from your room air. The refrigerant changes state — liquid to gas and back — to move heat from a cold space to a warmer one, which is why it needs work input (the compressor) to run "uphill" against natural heat flow.

How each one actually works

Non-inverter (fixed-speed) AC: the compressor motor is switched by a simple contactor straight onto mains supply, so it can only run at one speed — full capacity — or be off. A thermostat compares room temperature to the setpoint and simply turns the compressor ON when the room is too warm and OFF once it overshoots slightly below setpoint. This produces a temperature saw-tooth of roughly ±2–3°C, a large inrush current every time the motor starts (often 3–5× running current), and more mechanical wear from frequent starts/stops.

Inverter AC: the compressor is driven by a Variable Frequency Drive (VFD) — a small power-electronics module that rectifies incoming AC to DC and then re-synthesises it as AC at a controllable frequency and voltage. Since compressor (motor) speed is proportional to supply frequency, the VFD can run the compressor anywhere from roughly 20% to 120% of its rated speed. A microcontroller continuously compares room temperature to setpoint and throttles the compressor up or down smoothly, so the room glides to the setpoint and then holds there with the compressor "idling" at low speed rather than switching off — giving ±0.5°C stability, soft starts with negligible inrush, and much higher efficiency at partial load, which is where an AC spends most of its life. The trade-off is a more complex, costlier PCB/IPM (Intelligent Power Module) and compressor.

What is a "Ton" of Refrigeration?

The Ton of Refrigeration (TR) is a historical unit from the days before mechanical refrigeration, when buildings were cooled with blocks of ice. One ton of refrigeration is defined as the rate of heat absorption needed to melt one short ton (2000 lb / ~907 kg) of ice at 0°C in 24 hours.

1 TR  =  3.517 kW  =  3,517 W  =  12,000 BTU/hr  =  3,024 kcal/hr

So a "1.5 Ton AC" doesn't weigh 1.5 tons or use 1.5 tons of anything — it means the unit can remove heat at the same rate as melting 1.5 tons of ice per day, i.e. about 5,275 W (18,000 BTU/hr) of cooling capacity.
